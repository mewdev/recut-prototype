# Energy Arcs and Density

How energy should move across a sequence of cut sections — this is the direct answer to "how do I sequence sections for a satisfying shape."

## Energy is multi-dimensional, not just loudness

| Parameter | Lower energy | Higher energy |
|---|---|---|
| Dynamics | Softer | Louder |
| Density | Fewer/sparser parts | More/denser parts |
| Register | Lower/narrower | Higher/wider |
| Rhythm | Slower/sparser | Faster/denser |
| Harmony | Simpler/diatonic | More chromatic/tense |
| Timbre | Mellow/dark | Bright/harsh |
| Vocal intensity | Restrained | Belting, ad-libs |

The map gives you `loudness_db`/`loudness_db_start`/`loudness_db_end` as the one directly-measurable proxy. Treat "the loudest section" as a starting hypothesis, not the automatic answer for "the highest-energy section" — quiet moments make loud moments matter, and a section can be dense/high-register at moderate loudness.

## Density scale (0–10) and typical pop arc

| Density | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Silence to one sustained instrument |
| 3–4 | Small, restrained ensemble |
| 5–6 | Full band, moderate intensity |
| 7–8 | Full band, high energy — typical chorus peak |
| 9–10 | Maximum layering, near/at overload |

Typical pop density arc by section: `intro 2-3 → verse 4-5 → pre-chorus 5-6 → chorus 7-8 → verse2 4-5 → pre-chorus 5-6 → chorus 7-8 → bridge 3-5 → final chorus 8-9 → outro 4-2`. Use this as a prior for what a "normal" energy shape looks like when judging whether an assembled short edit's arc is plausible — not a rule to force.

## Named arc shapes — pick the target shape deliberately

- **Single arch**: rises once, peaks, falls. Simplest; good for a short highlight clip built around one chorus.
- **Multiple peaks**: each chorus is a peak, final peak highest. The standard full-song shape — if your short edit keeps 2+ choruses, this is what you're implicitly building.
- **Build-and-drop**: sustained tension release into a peak (EDM-style). Only apply this framing if the source material actually has a build section (`inst` with rising density) — don't force EDM logic onto a verse-chorus pop song.
- **Plateau-step**: each kept section is a step up from the last, no big single peak. Works well for a short edit that just wants forward momentum without a climax.
- **Frame**: high-low-high — open strong, drop to something contemplative, return strong. Good for a teaser that wants to show range.

## Sequencing rules that transfer directly

- **Density should always be moving.** Two adjacent kept sections at the same density level read as "stuck," even if the actual audio content differs.
- **Don't open with your densest section if you want a later peak to register.** Starting at chorus-level density leaves nowhere to go — the chorus won't read as an arrival.
- **A short edit's climax should sit around 70–80% of the way through**, not at the very start or dead center, if you want it to read as a climax rather than just "the loud part."
- **Listener fatigue is real**: ~30s at maximum density is comfortable, 60s starts to push attention, 90s+ and the listener tunes out — relevant when deciding how long a kept high-energy section should run in a short edit.

## Quick reference

| Want | Sequence |
|---|---|
| Short highlight clip, one big moment | Single arch: lower-density lead-in → the strongest chorus/hook → quick release |
| Compressed full-song feel | Multiple peaks: verse(lower) → chorus(peak) → verse(lower, can differ from V1) → chorus(peak, equal or higher) |
| Teaser/preview | Frame: strong open → brief contrast → strong close |
| Forward-moving edit, no dramatic climax | Plateau-step: each kept section slightly denser than the last |
