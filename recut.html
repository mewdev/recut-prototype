You speak human.
Recut speaks music.

You don't need to know what a cadence is. The LLM analysis layer reads your intent and translates it into a precise musical pipeline — then hands that to the cutting agent.

you say
"make it punchy, about 30 seconds"
→
recut understands
intent High energy, compact. Prioritise the most energetic section. Hard cut is acceptable.
target 28–32s · start at first energy peak · end at perfect authentic cadence
primitives cutAtSection("chorus", 1) → fadeAtCadence("bar_end")
you say
"I need a version that builds up slowly and ends on a high"
→
recut understands
intent Ascending energy arc. Preserve intro + verse. End at highest energy section.
target Full intro → verse → final chorus · end borrowed from outro cadence
primitives keepSections(["intro","verse","chorus_last"]) → borrowEndingCadence("outro")
you say
"cut out the slow middle part, keep the energy going"
→
recut understands
intent Remove low-energy section. Maintain momentum across the join.
analysis Bridge detected at 1:12 (energy 0.31). Non-linear join required — harmonic neutral point found at bar 24 beat 3.
primitives removeBridgeCleanly() → crossfadeAtHarmonicNeutral(800)
// architecture

The map is
the contract.

Recut decouples understanding from execution. Analysis tools hear the music. The LLM reasons over structure. A specialized model has taste. ffmpeg cuts.

01
Analysis Pipeline
Multiple tools — librosa, Essentia, chord detectors — run in parallel. A reconciliation layer resolves conflicts and outputs a unified JSON music map with candidate cut points.
02
JSON Music Map
The core artifact. Sections, beats, chord progressions, cadence types, energy curves, phrase boundaries. Every field earned by a real editing use case.
03
LLM Reasoning
A general LLM receives the map + user intent in natural language. It selects cuts and frames creative decisions — grounded by the map, not hallucinating timestamps.
04
Cutting Instruction Layer
A deterministic compiler translates LLM selections into validated, executable primitives. Catches errors before they reach audio.
05
Specialized Cutting Model
Trained on (map, instruction, good cut) triplets. This is where musical taste lives — not just technical correctness.

// cutting primitives

Musical operations,
not timestamps.

Pure functions over the music map. Every primitive knows what it means musically — not just where to cut.

Tier 1 — Standard
cutAtSection("chorus", 1)
Cut precisely at the boundary of a named section. Respects phrase structure.
Tier 1 — Standard
fadeAtCadence(map, "last_chorus")
Fade out at the nearest harmonically resolved cadence point.
Tier 1 — Standard
loopSection("verse", 2)
Seamlessly repeat a section N times using harmonic equivalence.
Tier 2 — Non-linear
borrowEndingCadence("outro", "chorus_2")
Graft the final cadence of one section onto another. The hard case.
Tier 2 — Non-linear
removeBridgeCleanly(map)
Remove bridge without energy drop — finds the smoothest transition point.
Tier 3 — Creative
makeRadioEdit("3:00")
Intelligently compress a track to target duration while keeping it punchy.

Who needs
musical cuts.

Any pipeline where music is currently handled dumbly — cut by timestamp, not by meaning.

Sora, Runway, Kling — scene cuts against musical phrase boundaries, not random frames.
high impact
🎙️
Podcast & Video Editors
Descript, Adobe Podcast — music bed cuts that don't glitch when you remove a segment.
immediate pain
📢
Ad Creative Platforms
15s/30s/60s versions of any track, generated at scale without a music editor.
high value
🎵
Sync Licensing
Artlist, Epidemic Sound — catalogue-scale versioning. Every track gets clean length variants.

🎮
